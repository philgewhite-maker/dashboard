Switch bonus requirements: the money tests explained

Most switch offers do not pay on opening alone. They ask you to move money through the new account in a particular way. This guide explains each kind of test and shows the figures for every offer we track.

The kinds of test

Across the 10 offers we track, you will meet these:

  • Salary: pay arriving from an employer. 4 of the 10 offers have one.
  • Pay-in: a set amount paid in, from £1,000 to £15,000 across the offers that have one (6 offers).
  • Direct Debits: a number moved across (covered in its own guide).
  • Debit card payments: a count of purchases, often every month for a period.
  • App log-in: a one-off login to the bank’s app.

A test almost always has a clock on it: “within 30 days of opening”, or “each month for five months”. Starting the clock too early, before the Direct Debits and payments are ready, is the most common reason a bonus is missed.

Salary tests

A salary test asks for pay arriving from an employer. The terms usually say what counts: typically a payment by Bacs from an employer, and sometimes a pension or benefits payment. They often say that a transfer from your own accounts does not count, which is why it matters to read the wording rather than assume.

Pay-in tests

A pay-in test asks for a minimum amount to arrive in the account, often within a number of days of opening or of the switch. How the money may be sourced varies by offer. Some terms accept a transfer from an account you hold elsewhere; others specify an employer or a third party. The exact wording is shown on each offer page.

A pay-in is a test of money arriving, not of what you earn. Whether your own money moved from another account satisfies it depends entirely on the offer’s wording, so read it before relying on that.

How long the money has to stay

Some offers say a deposit has to remain in the account for a set time, often a day or more, before it counts. Others say nothing. Where the terms are silent we say “not stated”, which means the offer does not tell you, not that there is no rule. Keep a record of when payments arrive and leave, and check the terms before you move anything back.

Bonuses paid in instalments

Several offers pay part of the bonus up front and the rest over later months, on the condition that you keep meeting a test, such as salary or card payments, in each of those months.

  • Barclays Premier: £450 and 5 × £30, £600 in all
  • Barclays Bank Account: £150 and 5 × £30, £300 in all

Take care to ask whether the later instalments are worth the commitment. A salary test over six months means your pay is tied to that account for that long.

A worked timeline

Here is how it looks for the HSBC Bank Account, using its own figures:

  1. Day 0: open the account.
  2. Before the switch date: make sure the 2 Direct Debits are in place on the account you are leaving.
  3. About 7 working days later: the switch completes and the Direct Debits arrive.
  4. The pay-in: £2,000 within 60 days.
  5. The bonus: £220, paid within 60 days of the conditions being met.

The timeline is illustrative. The offer’s own terms govern the dates.

Common questions

Does the money I pay in have to be new money?

That depends on the offer. Some terms say salary or a transfer from outside the bank is needed; others accept a transfer from an account of your own. The offer’s own wording decides, and we show it on each offer page. If the terms are unclear, ask the bank in writing before you rely on it.

Can I move the money out again afterwards?

Often, once the conditions are met and any “must stay” period has passed. Offers differ, and some ask for the account to be kept open for a period or for payments to recur, so check before moving it.

What if I miss a month of a salary test?

It depends. Some offers let you qualify for later instalments even if you miss a month; others do not. The wording for each is on its offer page.

Do I need a salary to get a switch bonus?

Not for all of them: 6 of the 10 offers we track have no salary test. They may still ask for a pay-in or Direct Debits.
